## Claim discrepancy

Define qualifying planning access as orderable fixed terrestrial service of at least 100 Mbps download and 20 Mbps upload. An existing terrestrial-coded block contributes its full household count to the baseline proxy, an explicit scenario because the file lacks address-level service tiers or installability. Satellite counts in raw provider coverage and is excluded from the target for terrestrial expansion. This choice does not imply satellite cannot meet a household's needs or the speed benchmark. In the rule test, use raw flags including satellite. The U.S. federal reference supplies the speed levels only. [FCC, 2024 Section 706 benchmark](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-401205A1.pdf).

North Ridge has 2,135 households, 1,177 subscriptions (55.13%) and 17 reported-served blocks containing 1,908 households (89.37%). Those blocks contain 1,531 satellite-coded households (13 blocks) and 377 terrestrial-coded households (17.66% of the township, 4 blocks). The terrestrial groups comprise cable (68 households), fiber (130), and fixed wireless (179). Three blocks containing 227 households are already map-unserved. The map treatment and the terrestrial proxy differ by 1,531 households, or 71.71 percentage points. Satellite-coded blocks have 854 subscriptions (55.78%) and terrestrial-coded blocks 212 (56.23%). Those aggregate subscriptions cannot identify which addresses have qualifying access. The named national cable provider's detailed claim and location-level overlaps are unavailable. The single cable-coded block has 68 households and cannot corroborate broader provider claims or complaints.

In North Ridge's satellite-coded blocks, 2.61 of 25.61 surveyed road miles (10.19%) recorded signal at or below −110 dBm, versus 0 of 3.40 cable-coded miles. The satellite-coded share is 22.88% at −105 dBm and 0.20% at −115 dBm. Surveyed road miles weight the segment diagnostic; neither denominator is a share of households. The measured technology, band, transmitter and sampling full coverage are unknown. Signal only motivates field investigation, including in cable-coded blocks with apparently stronger road signals.

Let S = 1,531 be the number of households in satellite-coded North Ridge blocks. Scenario reclassification is nearest whole household to qS (half up), where q is the share eventually verified and recognized as lacking 100/20 terrestrial access. Zero, one quarter, one half and all produce 0, 383, 766 and 1,531 respectively. The 0–1,531 range is a conditional identification interval for the satellite-focused inquiry, with 766 used only as a middle stress test. It concerns potential terrestrial reclassification, not cancellation of true satellite availability. Errors in the four terrestrial-coded blocks are outside this scenario and cannot be quantified from the CSVs. For the broader set of map-served block households, threshold-compliant service cannot be observed and only the trivial 0–1,908 bound is supported without added evidence or assumptions. Do not interpret the scenario interval as statistical confidence.

## Accepting the maps versus challenging

Accepting the existing map implies no incremental challenge expense, no map-related delay and zero corrected households. All six townships remain ineligible. Incorrect served marks can block an entire township even when a small subset has qualifying service. Residents retain current choices, the cooperative cannot obtain grants for expansion, the cable provider avoids a verification burden and subsidized rivals, and the county preserves administrative funds while funds for qualifying deployment remain blocked.

For one desk-based challenge round, budget 128 hours for address-level queries (about five minutes per candidate household if each corresponds to one address), 32 hours for reconciliation and preparation, 24 hours of follow-up, and $500 incidental cash. At the U.S. state and local government compensation benchmark of $65.68 per hour, this totals $12,585.12. Halving or doubling the scenario's hours and direct cash gives $6,292.56–$25,170.24. This is a modeling assumption, with a measured national labor-rate benchmark. Substantial field engineering would need a separate quote. The base uses a separate administrative appropriation. If these costs are paid from the deployment budget, subtract them before award. [BLS, December 2025 Employer Costs for Employee Compensation](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/ecec_03202026.htm).

Plan 30 days for local preparation. Use an analogous 60-day response, possible additional 60-day resolution, up to 90 days adjudication and 30 days for revision: 120 days from the start of preparation in the concession scenario and 270 in the fully contested scenario, excluding intake review and publication lag. Local adoption is a policy assumption. Identify disputed providers and services by address, collect documented order denials, dated quotes and installation outcomes, and let providers respond. A sample can identify priorities and shape inference; it cannot alone establish each challenged location. Record confirmed satellite availability even when the planning rule considers the address terrestrial-unserved. [47 CFR § 1.7006 (2024 edition)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2024-title47-vol1/pdf/CFR-2024-title47-vol1-sec1-7006.pdf). [FCC BDC fixed challenge evidence guidance](https://help.bdc.fcc.gov/hc/en-us/articles/12000296843291-How-to-Submit-a-Successful-Fixed-Availability-Challenge).

Even a successful satellite-only challenge leaves the four North Ridge terrestrial-coded blocks untouched. None of the challenge outcomes changes current strict-rule spending or deployment. With a block- or address-level rule or approved evidence override, upheld cases could admit up to 1,531 additional households to a future allocation process, while actual deployment depends on award and design. Residents must provide evidence and may face delays or data-sharing burdens; confirmed corrections provide a route to public investment after rule reform. The cooperative gains the opportunity to bid while taking on remote service and operating obligations. The national cable provider incurs verification and possible rebuttal expense, can substantiate valid claims, and may face subsidized competition in corrected locations. The county incurs challenge costs and gains an evidence base for requesting reform and targeting funds.
