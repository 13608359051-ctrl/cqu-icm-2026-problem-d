## Definitions

The 2026 planning snapshot uses the supplied synthetic data, whose observation year is unspecified. New qualifying access requires orderable fixed terrestrial 100/20 Mbps service. A wireline or terrestrial fixed wireless coverage claim contributes the entire block to baseline `A_t` in the nominal scenario. This is an optimistic within-claim planning proxy. Baseline service speeds, availability at individual locations, additional unreported access and actual uptake remain unidentified from the inputs. Satellite served flags count in the strict rule and satellite does not satisfy the terrestrial planning target. Subscription counts and road signals provide no service tier or installation capacity and do not enter the project gains.

For township `t`, `H_t` is household count, `M_t = ceil(floor * H_t)` is its minimum, and `S_t` is the sum of raw provider-served block indicators. A candidate project `i=(b,k)` chooses block `b` and technology `k`, F or W, with binary decision `x_i`, lifecycle cost `c_i` and incremental qualifying household opportunities `a_i`. The strict eligibility flag is `e_b = 1[S_t=0]`; the block amendment uses `e_b = 1[served_b=0]`. A separate verified-correction case also enables the 13 North Ridge satellite-coded blocks only on the counterfactual that county address-level evidence establishes absence of qualifying terrestrial service there and the funding authority accepts it. Claims at other blocks remain as recorded. North Ridge is the only explicitly Northern township identifiable in the dataset.

Each block has one option at most: `sum(i at b) x_i <= e_b`. The code omits ineligible options. Budget is `sum(i) c_i x_i <= B` in 2026 dollars PV. Equity is `A_t + sum(i in t) a_i x_i >= M_t` for every township. Maximize `sum(i) a_i x_i` so each newly serviceable household has equal weight, then minimize the lifecycle resource use while holding the optimal household count. This avoids requiring arbitrary conversions between dollars and coverage. A separate solve minimizes cost while meeting the floors, with the same binary menu. The base model has at most 94 binary options (47 blocks times two technologies), or 120 after the North verification assumption, solvable with deterministic MILP and no data sampling.

## Cost and coverage coefficients

Let `d_b` be the reported distance to the backbone in miles, and `q_b = terrain_b - 1` in 0–4. Fiber offers `a_bF = H_b` under the design assumption of passing the full currently eligible block. Fixed wireless offers `a_bW = floor(H_b * p_q)` with `p_q = (0.80,0.75,0.70,0.62,0.55)`. This pass fraction covers the scenario's limitations of terrain, sufficient signal and service capacity. It does not use road dBm as proof of fixed wireless availability. The underlying scenario assumes suitable existing vertical assets and wireless relay capacity. Validate tower rights, radio paths, spectrum, upgrade capability and load before funding. Model geography is an independent connection per block to a generic existing backbone plus Task 3's hypothesized nodes. Backbone distance functions as a route-length proxy. A block designation does not imply a tower site or actual route.

| Cost component | Fiber | Fixed wireless |
|---|---|---|
| Initial backhaul | `42240 * d_b * (1 + 0.16 q_b)` | `5500 * d_b * (1 + 0.10 q_b)` |
| Initial last mile | `980 * H_b * (1 + 0.10 q_b)` | `28000 * (1 + 0.08 q_b) + 480 * a_bW` |
| Annual O&M | `0.018 * total capex` | `0.035 * total capex` |

Round each initial cost to the nearest dollar first. Multiply the sum of initial costs by the technology-specific rate and the annuity factor `sum(y=1..10)(1.03)^(-y)=8.530202837`, round O&M PV, and add the result to the rounded capex. This reserves the modeled O&M amount up front and does not forecast retail receipts or take-up, lease success, inflation, residual value, or provider break-even. Year 0 project capex and 10 years of O&M are common to all mechanisms. The sources and assumptions are in `sources/task2_parameters.csv`. The Nebraska overview provides an order-of-magnitude deployment comparator (total about USD 3,168 per eligible location before O&M) and is not used to estimate Hallowell's budget. Hallowell is fictional and household opportunities are not interchangeable with Nebraska BSLs.

## Feasibility

- North Ridge / East Corridor / South Hollow / West Plains / Central Town / River Bend have 17 / 12 / 13 / 7 / 15 / 9 raw served blocks. All townships have strict eligibility zero. Removing only the floor leaves a feasible zero-project solution with zero spending, which establishes that the budget and strict eligibility are jointly satisfiable. Keeping the 25% floor leaves deficits of 157 / 0 / 0 / 120 / 0 / 204. Additional budget cannot cure them while eligibility is zero.
- Block-level eligibility alone unlocks 47 blocks (5,414 households). At the 25% floor the plan gains 4,213 opportunities for $10,980,237, uses 12 fiber and 32 wireless projects, and achieves a minimum township rate of 28.10%. The cost-minimum way to satisfy the floors adds 526 opportunities from five wireless projects for $793,458. That is a distinct minimum-cost demonstration.
- With a 50% floor and block-level eligibility alone, North Ridge reaches at most 377 + 227 = 604 qualifying opportunities against 1,068 required. Amending eligibility and verifying all 13 North satellite-coded blocks in the counterfactual makes 50% feasible. Cost minimization returns 2,930 gains and $4,969,620, while coverage maximization returns 5,311 and $10,994,761. Removing the stronger floor to 25% in the same verified case leaves that direct benchmark's optimum unchanged. The feasibility gain depends on approved evidence and the rule change, since North Ridge still has four flagged terrestrial blocks.
- If only 75% of the household locations in each terrestrial-claimed block are baseline-qualifying, rounded down by block, the 25% block-amended case is infeasible. For North Ridge the upper bound is 282 + 227 = 509, below its obligation of 534. At least 307 qualifying baseline locations are necessary in North Ridge for any block-amended plan with a 25% floor, since only 227 newly eligible locations are available before verification.

## Sensitivity and verification

Within the one-at-a-time ranges tested, `wireless_terrain_pass_pct` most affects total added access (3,892–4,553; span 661), followed by the budget stress (3,836–4,434; span 598), fiber backhaul rate (4,047–4,342; span 295), and O&M stress (4,164–4,259; span 95). The baseline stress changes feasibility, a stronger qualitative effect. Wireless reachability moves the fiber/wireless mix from 13/29 to 10/36. Fiber backhaul changes the mix from 16/27 at aerial Q1 to 7/38 at the underground median, indicating stronger impact on technology choice than the W pass shift in these ranges. The block-level identity changes are shown in the project results. In East Corridor, block 420010000027 substitutes fiber for wireless under low fiber cost. With better wireless passing, East Corridor 420010000024 substitutes wireless for fiber. These are planning block selections, not surveyed construction routes. All feasible sweeps in this paragraph meet the 25% floor.
