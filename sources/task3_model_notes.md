# Procurement and usability evidence

## Common comparison

Each model uses a $11 million gross 2026-dollar lifecycle ceiling, a ten-year horizon and a 3% real discount rate. The planning service is fixed terrestrial 100/20 Mbps. The three procurement menus face the same eligibility and 25% per-town floor in each regime. The floor uses the integer ceiling of one quarter of township households. Baseline access is a conditional proxy based on terrestrial-coded blocks. Provider claims, subscription rates and field-proven qualifying service are different quantities.

- **Strict rule.** All six townships have at least one marked served block. All three award mechanisms permit zero deployment, and the equity-constrained model is infeasible. The unchanged township ban would still apply after deleting only the 13 northern satellite flags, because four other North Ridge flags remain. The main operating comparison therefore requires an approved block-based pilot.
- **Block pilot.** Only original map-unserved blocks can receive support. The full cooperative award makes the same candidate actions and costs available as in Task 2(a). The cooperative receives the sole contract with the remaining budget as its ceiling. The unspent balance stays in reserve. In the tender, each conforming offer covers a block and technology within the specified bidder footprint. Lowest lifecycle dollars per new household wins among offers with equal delivery. Across a portfolio, maximum incremental coverage comes first and lowest total requested dollars breaks ties. At a fixed total number of newly passed households, this also minimizes average dollars per household.
- **Verified county override.** With the pilot in place, authorize affected North Ridge addresses only after accepted address-level proof shows unavailable qualifying terrestrial service. The model simulates successful certification of all 13 satellite-coded North Ridge blocks. Provider-marked terrestrial blocks and other townships' satellite-only claims retain their original statuses. This scenario extends eligibility without adding any baseline households.

## County partnership

The county opens at least one fiber hub in the partnership, with at least two funded client blocks connected to every opened hub. The same budget can also fund conforming direct projects where leasing is unattractive. One notional hub for township \(j\) uses the median backbone-distance \(d_j\) and median terrain \(t_j\) from that township's original map-unserved blocks. Its capex is \(25{,}000+42{,}240d_j[1+0.16(t_j-1)]\), rounded to dollars. It carries the shared annual fiber O&M rate of 1.8% of capex. Each private leased action retains a lateral backhaul segment of modeled length \(|d_i-d_j|+0.5\) miles. Its Task 2 distance-based backhaul capex is scaled by this length divided by original \(d_i\), followed by the common tech-specific O&M rate. Lessee technology, reach caps and equipment reuse appear in the parameter sheet. The distances are a schematic routing proxy because no coordinates, road alignment, spectrum, capacity or physical interconnection routes were supplied.

Let \(x_a\) indicate adoption of a block action and \(y_j\) opening of a hub. Only one action may serve a block. A local action satisfies \(x_a\le y_j\), and the sum across supported client actions of an open hub is at least \(2y_j\). The modeled total hub cost enters the gross budget once. Publish the interconnection terms, use a common recurring fee for similarly situated lessees and require site and capacity studies before committing to construction. The Kansas example supports a carrier-neutral structure, while Hallowell's terms remain simulated. [Kansas Department of Commerce, Freestate middle-mile rates and access](https://www.kansascommerce.gov/program/challenge/middle-mile/)

The county owns hub capex and maintenance. Providers own their assigned local capital and operating obligations and pay each signed provider-hub lease. Rates are booked as county receipts and equally as private payments. They do not fund extra gross deployment or change the dispatch objective. With seven contracts at $4,800 per contract-year, modeled receipts are $286,615 PV. Under the block pilot, hub O&M is $331,596 PV, leaving $44,981 PV to reserve. Under the verified scenario, the reserve gap is $3,913 PV. O&M-only break-even fees are about $5,553 and $4,866 per contract-year, respectively. This operating ratio excludes recovery of capital and provider retail economics. At zero paying leases the county must support all hub O&M. Require enforceable lease commitments, a funded maintenance account, and cost and cash projections including downstream demand, customer support, replacement risk and total affordability before awarding.

Full outcomes are in the six Task 3 result tables. PPP adds 552 more households than the sole cooperative in the block pilot, and 439 more in the verified scenario. In the base block pilot, model assignments are 3,760 new households through the national cable operator and 1,005 through the fixed-wireless entrant. The cooperative may lease on the same terms, but receives no assignment in that particular optimum. It is the sole recipient under its 4,213-household alternative, and secures 349 modeled PPP households in the verified scenario. County taxpayers gain coverage per dollar under the pilot but carry hub asset, outage and occupancy risks. A sole award gives the cooperative buildout and operating accountability without shared public sites, while the national cable operator cannot compete for that single contract. Bidding offers entry on lower-cost blocks, but costs and footprint reach leave fewer households covered. These are hypothetical supplier offers, derived from technology flags only as screening proxies.

## Benefit and actual use

Use incremental distinct households passed by a qualifying network as the model benefit. The associated household fraction is an additional distribution measure. Under the recommended block-pilot PPP, West Plains gains 1,367 households (65.22% of its 2,096 households). Removing only the 25% floor preserves the solution, because every post-build rate in the unconstrained solution already reaches at least 27.54%. The strongest benefit shifts to North Ridge after the separate verification policy takes effect (1,675 additional households, 78.45% of its 2,135 households).

The South Hollow CSV supplies 398 of 1,812 households reporting any subscription (21.96%), including 99 of 491 on five fixed-wireless-claimed blocks (20.16%), versus 299 of 1,321 households on other Southern blocks (22.63%). The 99 count is an upper bound on subscriptions using the claimed technology among those 491 households if all such subscriptions are counted. The data provide no matching tower footprint, provider-specific subscriptions, peak loading, failure logs, reasons for nonadoption or daytime device usage. The scenario description reports idle stations, which could coincide with off-peak consumption, inadequate capacity when users need it, or barriers to subscription.

Measure three rates together, with published denominators and confidence intervals:

1. **Orderability.** Fraction of sampled intended households quoted a timely standard installation of qualifying service. Log eligible address matches, order attempts, fees, delays, cancellations and failures, including non-subscribers.
2. **Take-up and retention.** Active accounts among independently orderable locations. Observe churn, retail and equipment charges, delinquency, failed installs and safer aggregate enrollment records. Stratify by geography, income and presence of students, workers and seniors.
3. **Work- and school-ready service.** Among intended households, estimate the fraction able to order, afford and successfully complete a representative class call, assignment upload or work video/VPN task at the expected times. Check both wired gateway tests and end-device Wi-Fi to isolate in-home faults. Measure repeated download and upload throughput, latency under load, packet loss, outage minutes and task completion in work, after-school and evening peak windows, in representative seasons and weather. Track tower-level complaints, capacity and uplink hours by sector. Start pre-award, then repeat after 90 days and each quarter. Publish privacy-preserving aggregates. [FCC fixed broadband measurement report](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/FCC-24-136A6_Rcd.pdf) · [CRTC fixed wireless field measurements](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/publications/reports/rp240530/rp240530.htm)

Survey connected and unconnected households about desired tasks, alternative service, willingness to pay, equipment, accessibility, language and digital skills. Use provider broadband labels to identify base price, post-promotion price, fees, data limits and performance. Among serviceable non-subscribers, pilot staggered or randomized offers of fee relief and separate assisted installation, devices or skills support. Compare the change in verified enrollment and successful task use across offer groups against a scheduled comparison group. A material response to removal of a barrier supports an access explanation. Stable low interest despite functional, affordable service and credible alternatives is evidence of lower unmet demand. Small samples, nonresponse and weak treatment should preclude a firm causal conclusion. In East Corridor, the modeled pilot hub serves seven client blocks and the township gains 999 residential passes across its full portfolio. A later, separately funded logistics-site Wi-Fi or anchor-service pilot should require a workforce survey by shift, a participating site and a signed anchor agreement. Commuters, anchor revenue and hotspot users are excluded from household and lease benefits in the model.
